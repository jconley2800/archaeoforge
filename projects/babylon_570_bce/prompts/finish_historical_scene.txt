1. Identity and moment

Create a lifelike archaeological reconstruction of Babylon at approximately 570 BCE, during the reign of Nebuchadnezzar II. Show a functioning, crowded imperial capital, not ruins. Hot late-afternoon light, dust, and slight heat haze are explicitly illustrative dry-season visual completion rather than a known weather observation for the depicted day.

Before output, enforce three non-negotiable visual checks: (1) there is exactly one blue Ishtar Gate, far north of Etemenanki, and the Processional Way visibly passes through its central portal; (2) a broad interval of palace, temple, Kadingirra, streets, and houses separates the gate and ziggurat; and (3) the south face of Etemenanki visibly has all three unobstructed lowest-stage approach flights in a T-shaped plan—one centred north-south flight forming the stem, plus a mirror-symmetrical pair of east-west lateral flights pressed directly along the facade forming the straight crossbar. Use an elevated enough view to show all three flights and their shared landing. The stair plan is T-shaped, never Y-shaped: do not omit the central or either lateral flight, and do not angle the lateral pair outward toward the viewer.

2. Reference role

Use Image 1, the supplied Blender beauty render, only as a schematic visual and compositional starting point. 3. Plan-led spatial correction and composition anchors

Follow the broad relationships shown in Olof Pedersén's archaeological Babylon Centre plan and the UNESCO Babylon documentation as source-led corrections that still require project review:

- Put the Euphrates west of the central palace and temple districts; do not inherit the base render's claim that the river is necessarily on the east or right side of the frame.
- Show the Ishtar Gate once, at the northern edge of the inner city beside the palace district. The South Palace complex lies immediately west and southwest of it, toward the Euphrates.
- Exactly one visibly blue monumental portal complex may appear anywhere in the image: the Ishtar Gate. Its smaller northern and larger southern gatehouses must read as contiguous parts of the same approximately 50-metre-deep complex, not as two distant gates. Do not create another blue portal, gatehouse, or gate-shaped shrine farther along the road, in the palace, on the city wall, or on Etemenanki.
- Center the Ishtar Gate directly across the north-south Processional Way. The roadway must enter the northern gatehouse, pass continuously through both gate courts and portals, and emerge from the southern gatehouse on the same ceremonial route. The gate must not sit beside, parallel to, or offset from its road.
- Place Etemenanki in its own sacred precinct roughly 800-850 metres south-southwest of the Ishtar Gate in plan, with a long interval of palace, temple, Kadingirra, street, and ordinary urban fabric between them. They are not adjacent, do not share an enclosure, and must not read as a gate-and-ziggurat complex. In this oblique composition, keep Etemenanki in the southern foreground or midground and move the gate far into the northern palace zone, where perspective makes it visibly smaller.
- Continue the Processional Way south from the Ishtar Gate through the city toward the Marduk sanctuary and Etemenanki precinct. Its celebrated approximately 180-metre glazed, lion-lined corridor is north of the gate; do not stretch blue ceremonial walls all the way to the ziggurat or overassert one ruler-straight road across archaeologically missing stretches.
- Keep the walled urban core dense around these separated anchors. If both the gate and Etemenanki cannot fit at credible scale and separation in the starter crop, widen the field of view rather than compressing the distance.

4. Ordinary built fabric

Replace the model blocks with dense, mostly one-storey flat-roofed courtyard houses, workshops, storerooms, and tall, mostly blank street walls. Use sun-dried mudbrick with earth-toned mud plaster; palm or poplar beams, slats, and reed mats beneath flat mud roofs; occasional white lime-gypsum plaster on important or prosperous buildings; and reddish or buff baked brick with black bitumen only at drains, wall bases, monuments, and water-contact zones. Streets and residential lanes are mostly irregular packed earth, commonly narrow, with selective brick or stone paving near royal, temple, and elite buildings rather than universal paving. Add sober wear, drainage, rooftop work, jars, awnings, and small shopfront activity as illustrative completion. Do not make Babylon a stone city.

5. Monuments

Depict the Ishtar Gate as one monumental, believable roofed double gate approximately 50 metres deep, with a smaller northern gatehouse and a larger southern gatehouse, crenellated towers, and its portals precisely aligned to the Processional Way. Concentrate deep cobalt-blue glazed baked brick and restrained rows of moulded muskhushshu dragons and aurochs on the upper royal gate treatment, using cream, yellow, white, black, and muted red accents. North of the gate, show the approximately 180-metre ceremonial corridor about 20 metres across, with a roughly 10-metre paved road of pale limestone centre slabs and reddish side bands, flanked by blue glazed-brick walls with repeated nearly life-size striding lions. Blue glaze is exceptional royal display, not an ordinary-city material. Do not invent a second blue gate near Etemenanki.

Depict Etemenanki conservatively as a seven-stage Mesopotamian ziggurat with an approximately 91.5-metre square footprint, a roughly 60-metre mudbrick core, a thick baked-brick mantle, seven visually countable progressively smaller terraces, and a restrained earth-toned summit sanctuary that cannot be mistaken for a blue city gate. Orient its south facade toward the camera and keep its forecourt clear so the attested tripartite lowest-stage approach is unmistakable. Build a clear T in plan: one broad central staircase projects perpendicular to the exact midpoint of the south facade and climbs from the southern foreground toward its central landing, forming the T's stem; two equally prominent, mirror-symmetrical lateral stair flights lie parallel to and directly against that same south facade, one running east-to-centre and one west-to-centre, forming the T's straight crossbar. All three complete flights and their shared meeting point must be simultaneously visible. The lateral flights must hug the facade from its two corners inward—not descend toward the foreground, not become two diagonal fan arms, and not replace the central staircase. Do not put grand stairs on any other face, wrap them around the side faces, or invent confident stair circulation on the uncertain upper stages. A total height around 90 metres is plausible but uncertain; keep the upper stages sober. It must read as a stepped temple tower, not an Egyptian pyramid, fantasy tower, or mountain.

Render the palace district as a large network of high mudbrick and baked-brick enclosure walls and internal courtyards between the river and gate, with selective glazed facades rather than a single white block.

6. Environment

Continue the city into a flat, hot, irrigated Mesopotamian alluvial plain. Concentrate reeds, date palms, and greener growth along the Euphrates, canals, and cultivation zones. Keep orchards and fields mainly beyond the tightly packed inner residential core, including open cultivation toward the outer eastern enclosure, rather than turning central Babylon into a lush urban garden. Use dusty golden air, natural earth colours, physically plausible shadows, atmospheric depth, and restrained haze.

7. Daily life

Populate the scene at believable architectural scale with many small inhabitants engaged in water carrying, portering, modest trade, workshop activity, guarding, worship, and household work. Dress ordinary people chiefly in plain draped rectangular wool wraps or blanket-garments and simple jerkin- or tunic-like garments in cream, tan, and brown, with only a few muted dyed or long fringed elite garments; exact cut and drape remain interpretive. Show pack donkeys, small groups of sheep or goats, cattle where useful, and at most one restrained service cart. At river landings show baskets, sacks, and jars of barley, dates, sesame, and sesame oil, with modest non-iconic cargo craft whose exact hull construction is uncertain. Avoid prominent chariots, mass camel caravans, or modern dhow rigs.

8. Uncertainty rules

The spatial correction is guided by Pedersén, Babylon Maps, Babylon100_Centre_6.2 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.13380012), and the UNESCO Babylon Management Plan, but it is not approved project geometry and the finished image must remain review-required and non-authoritative. Treat precise roof forms, everyday clothing cuts, boat construction, awnings, rooftop activity, exact weather, ziggurat upper stages, summit sanctuary, and upper stair circulation as restrained visual completion. Omit the Hanging Gardens: their location and even their attribution to Babylon are not securely established by Babylonian archaeological or epigraphic evidence.

9. Negative constraints

No Ishtar Gate adjacent to the ziggurat, inside or attached to the Etemenanki precinct, or sharing its plaza; no compressed monument cluster; no duplicate blue gate; no roadway missing or skirting the Ishtar Gate; no blue lion-lined avenue running directly to the ziggurat; no blue glaze across ordinary houses; no Hanging Gardens, waterfalls, lush fantasy terraces, domes, minarets, Greek or Roman columns, Egyptian motifs, medieval Islamic architecture, modern objects or boats, asphalt, glass, steel, oversized statues, mass camel caravans, war-chariot spectacle, fantasy megastructures, excessive tropical vegetation, ruins, excavation trenches, pristine theme-park CGI, labels, borders, logos, or watermarks.
