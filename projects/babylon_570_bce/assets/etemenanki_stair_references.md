# Etemenanki stair-orientation references

These files are supporting visual references for non-authoritative historical-scene finishing. They are not project geometry or approved evidence claims.

## `pedersen_babylon_centre_etemenanki_stairs_crop.png`

- Source: Olof Pedersén, *Babylon Maps*, `Babylon100_Centre_6.2.pdf` (Babylon Centre), DOI [10.5281/zenodo.13380012](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13380012).
- Crop: the Etemenanki plan, retained at source orientation to show the central southward-projecting stair and the west/east lateral stairs along the same south facade.
- Reuse: the Uppsala University [Model of Babylon downloads page](https://www.uu.se/en/department/archaeology-ancient-history-and-conservation/research/research-projects/model-of-babylon) states that the preliminary maps may be reused without further permission. Attribute Olof Pedersén and the DOI.
- Status here: source-led supporting reference; not ingested into the evidence register and not approved project geometry.

## `etemenanki_tripartite_stair_orientation.svg` and `.png`

- Purpose: an ArchaeoForge-authored orientation aid that isolates the capital-T plan visible in Pedersén's plan.

## `user_supplied_ziggurat_stairs_v12.png`

- Source: image supplied directly by the user on 2026-08-09 as a reference for correcting the visible stair structure.
- Purpose: visual-form reference for the physical depth, continuous risers, parapet edges, shared centre landing, and separate legibility of the three monumental stair flights.
- Governing limitation: retain the source-led capital-T plan from Pedersen's plan--one central southward-projecting stair and two lateral stairs lying along the south facade--while using this image to prevent the lateral flights from collapsing into flat terrace bands.
- Do not copy its isolated background, colour, decorative treatment, number or proportions of upper stages, or summit form.
- Status: user-supplied comparative visual completion only; not archaeological evidence, approved geometry, or an independent historical source.

## Textual cross-check

Pedersén, “The Tower of Babylon Stele Found in Babylon,” *Iraq* (2024), pp. 188–189, DOI [10.1017/irq.2024.4](https://doi.org/10.1017/irq.2024.4), describes three staircases on the south facade and is published under CC BY 4.0. It also cautions that parts of the central and left stair depiction on the stele are less well preserved; exact elevations and parapet details therefore remain uncertain.
